# Tide and Timber: Game Vision

Status: first agreed foundation; detailed story and systems design remain open.

## Purpose and authority

This is the master reference for the game's overarching story, tone, and
high-level mechanics. Agreed decisions here steer future story and game-design
decisions. More detailed documents will expand on this foundation as the design
develops; their structure is not decided yet.

Only sections marked as agreed are commitments. Proposals and open questions
are not canon or implementation requirements. Change agreed decisions explicitly
before introducing a conflicting design in another document.

The [working story outline](story-outline.md) proposes a more granular journey
from the first night on the beach through four engine-room dungeons to departure.
Its new details remain proposals and do not override the agreed sections here.

The [island map](island-map.md) illustrates that outline's proposed geography,
including stage locations, farming-gated routes, and buried engine rooms.

## Agreed: experience and presentation

- A single-player 2D pixel-art survival and life-sim game with a story that ends.
- A look and feel similar to Stardew Valley, with an identity of its own.
- The project's established emotional baseline is cozy, curious, and resilient.

## Agreed: premise and emotional direction

The protagonist is a castaway who washes up on an island with no memory of life
before arriving there. They are an ordinary person with no prior connection to
the ark's history, not a former member of its crew or a person created by it.
Their arrival, however, is not merely chance: the ark has drawn them to it.

The cause of the memory loss and whether memories are eventually recovered
remain undecided.

The opening is about surviving and beginning to build a home. As the game
progresses, the player discovers more of the island and learns that they are not
alone: other people and creatures live there.

The player's growing motivation is to build a home, solve the island's mystery,
and discover what lies beyond. Returning to a remembered pre-island life is not
the central motivation.

## Agreed: the ark's call

Through an unseen power, the ark sensed that the protagonist had the right
heart and mind to defeat the darkness and drew them to the island.

The story should convey, at some level, that the protagonist is "the chosen
one". They remain an ordinary person, chosen for their qualities rather than
a hidden identity as a former gardener or a being created by the ark. This
connection complements the life they build and the skills they learn on the
island.

How the power works, how the player senses this connection, and when they
understand it remain undecided. The ark's role in drawing the protagonist does
not yet establish whether it caused the shipwreck or the memory loss.

## Agreed: the central revelation

The island is an ancient ark: what appears to be an ordinary island is actually
a vessel.

Who made the ark, which ecosystems it originally carried, why it appears to be
an island, and how much its inhabitants know about its nature remain undecided.

The story leads to an unexpected endgame in which the island resumes its journey
as an ark. The exact destination and final sequence remain to be designed.

## Agreed: the wandering garden

The ark was built to carry a living ecosystem between worlds and restore life
to places where it has disappeared.

The ark became stranded on Earth after a darkness infiltrated it. Restoring its
ability to fulfil its original purpose is the overarching story objective.

## Agreed: the darkness and central conflict

The darkness has taken over the ark's inner machinery and parts of the
overworld. It also spawns dark creatures, providing the story reason for
fighting monsters.

The player must find various remedies to push back and ultimately vanquish the
darkness. Doing so enables the ark to operate as intended.

Combat lets the player reach a local source of darkness; a remedy developed
through farming provides the lasting cure. Defeating dark creatures alone does
not restore an infected area. Farming and fighting therefore remain necessary
parts of progression.

Completed restorations are permanent. The darkness does not reclaim restored
areas, including through story events. Progress is not a recurring maintenance
or territory-defence obligation.

The darkness's origin and nature, the specific remedies, and the individual
restoration encounters remain to be designed.

## Agreed: the island community

The player discovers an established village with its own traditions and an
incomplete understanding of the island.

The community exists before the protagonist arrives. Its knowledge and customs
provide a connection to the island's past without providing a complete
explanation of the ark.

The individual inhabitants, specific traditions, and circumstances of the first
meeting remain to be designed.

## Agreed: high-level activities

- Survival and establishing a home are central to the opening.
- Farming and gathering are part of the game.
- Fighting monsters is part of the game.
- Discovering more of the island is part of progression.

Their detailed systems, difficulty, and pacing remain to be designed.

## Agreed: expedition-focused survival and farming progression

Survival is expedition-focused rather than a permanent struggle to maintain
basic needs. Establishing food, shelter, and tools matters early; the player
eventually develops a dependable home and prepares for more demanding journeys.
Exact needs, difficulty, and defeat penalties remain undecided.

The core progression alternates between expeditions and developing the home
farm. Farming is not merely an optional source of income or food: developing it
in various ways helps open new areas and dungeons.

Farm-developed remedies provide one connection between farming and access to
new places. The specific farming developments, their unlocks, and discoveries
brought back from expeditions remain to be designed.

Farming begins with familiar crops and methods. As the game progresses, it
gradually introduces extraordinary plants and ecosystems alongside the discovery
of the ark's nature.

## Agreed: the ending

After the darkness is vanquished and the ark is restored, the whole ark leaves
Earth with the player's home and the village community aboard. The farm and
village remain part of the ark; the finale does not discard them for a separate
spaceship.

A short epilogue shows life taking root on another world, fulfilling the
wandering garden's purpose. This is the conclusion of the story, not the start
of a second full world of progression.

The destination, the villagers' responses to departure, the final remedy, and
the staging of the launch and epilogue remain to be designed.

## Proposals: connecting ordinary life to the revelation

These suggestions remain open and need separate agreement:

- Restoring farms, habitats, and water systems also restores the ark's ability
  to sustain life and travel.
- Apparently natural places contain early clues to an artificial structure.
- The revelation changes the meaning of the player's accomplishments without
  erasing them.
- The final remedy brings together what the player has learned to cultivate.
- Home is protected from hostile attacks; danger is concentrated in exploration
  and expeditions. Expedition-focused survival is agreed, but the presence or
  absence of attacks on the home is not.

## Open questions

- What do the villagers know or believe, and how does the player meet them?
- What caused the protagonist's memory loss, and how important is recovering
  those memories to the story?
- How did the ark draw the protagonist to it, and did that cause the shipwreck
  or memory loss?
- How and when does the player sense that the ark chose them?
- Who built the ark, and how long has it been stranded on Earth?
- Where did the darkness come from, and what is its nature?
- Which remedies push back the darkness, and how are they discovered and used?
- Which farming developments and discoveries make each remedy possible?
- Which events motivate successive expeditions once immediate survival is secure?
- How are discoveries paced, particularly the revelation of the ark's nature
  and purpose?
- What does restoring or launching the ark require?
- How are the final restoration, launch, and new-world epilogue staged?
- What preparation do expeditions require, and what are the consequences of
  defeat?
- How does the village prepare for and respond to departure?
