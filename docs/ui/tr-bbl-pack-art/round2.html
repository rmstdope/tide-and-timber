Round 2 — the man's look, how he wakes, and the HUD

Clothes and hair in these drawings are painted automatically as rough stand-ins, so they'll look neater once they're painted by hand.

4 — What does the castaway look like?

Look 1 — dark hair and stubble, pale torn shirt, blue rolled trousers, bare feet (recommended)

Why: it keeps today's man's colours (pale shirt, blue trousers), so anyone who's played already knows him, and the hair makes him a person, not a mannequin.

Look 2 — the pack's bald head, shirt and trousers only

Less painting (clothes only), but he stays bald and faceless.

Look 3 — dark hair, no shirt (lost at sea), blue rolled trousers

Makes the shipwreck feel rougher. Less painting than Look 1, but there's more bare skin against the sand, so he's harder to spot.

5 — How does he get up on the beach?

This is the moment after the shipwreck, and every morning after he's collapsed.

Wake 1 — his collapse played backwards: lying down, pushes himself up, stands (recommended)

Why: waking mirrors collapsing, so the morning after reads as the answer to the night before, and no new art is needed.

Wake 2 — he's already standing when the picture fades in

Simplest, but the "I washed up here" moment disappears.

6 — How far does the HUD change?

This is the bar of 8 slots along the bottom. The icons get repainted in the pack's colours either way, and the lettering stays the same.

HUD 1 — same bar and shape, only the colours change to the pack's (recommended)

Why: the bar's layout, sizes and colour-blind marks were only just agreed. Changing only the colours keeps all of that intact.

HUD 2 — a wooden frame around the bar and slots, to match the pack's crates and workbench

Cosier, but it's a new drawn frame that has to scale to 1.5× cleanly, and the marks have to be rechecked on wood.
