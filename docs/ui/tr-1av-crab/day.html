By day: going about, claws up, digging in, coming back out

The crab scuttles about a five-tile stretch of its own, a tile or two at a time with pauses of one to three seconds. When the man is about four tiles away it stops and raises its claws, and holds them up while he stays there; if he backs off it lowers them and carries on. Within about two tiles it digs in, leaving a little heap of sand. About two seconds after he is more than five tiles away it climbs back out of the same spot and the heap goes.
